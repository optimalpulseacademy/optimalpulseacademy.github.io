Medical coding vs medical billing in Egypt: which path to choose? (2026)

Getting hired, pay inside Egypt, dollar-paid work with US clients, shifts and growth.

OptimalPulse Academy · Updated November 2026

Quick answer: Billing is usually the easier way in, and with a certificate and experience it can lead to contract work for US practices, paid in US dollars. Coding usually needs a CPC from day one and suits people who like quiet, detailed work. Learn the basics of both either way.

I get this question a lot, so here is an honest comparison from someone who holds a CPC and has worked in US medical billing for nearly 5 years.

What each job is

  • Coding: you read the doctor's notes and turn them into codes (ICD-10-CM, CPT, HCPCS). Quiet, focused work with documents.
  • Billing: you take those codes and get the claim paid: submitting claims, posting payments, following up unpaid claims, fixing and appealing denials. Insurance, money and problem-solving.

Getting your first job in Egypt

  • Billing is usually the easier way in. Many companies in Cairo and Giza hire AR callers, payment posters and charge entry staff with good English and basic training. Experience can come first and certification later.
  • Coding jobs usually ask for a CPC certificate, often with experience in a specialty. Some coding jobs are for Gulf insurance, not only US, which is a different system to learn.

In both, what raises your pay is the same: strong English, specialty experience and a recognised certificate.

The bigger opportunity: working directly with US clients

Once you are certified and experienced, billing opens another door. You can work as an independent contractor through freelance platforms such as Upwork, or through LinkedIn, and be paid in US dollars.

To be honest, these opportunities are not easy to find. For many years, the US outsourcing market has relied on long-established teams in India, Pakistan and the Philippines. They built a strong track record, large teams and competitive rates, so US practices know and trust them.

That is starting to change. US practices are beginning to notice Egyptian billers as a newer talent pool. Egypt's working hours also overlap more with the US East Coast than most outsourcing hubs, which helps with live calls and same-day follow-up.

How to stand out as a newcomer

  1. A recognised certificate (CPB or CPC), so a client who has never hired from Egypt has a reason to trust you.
  2. Real, specific experience: the specialties, systems and insurers you have actually worked with.
  3. Reliability: clear communication, on-time reports and HIPAA-safe habits. That is how a newer market earns repeat clients.

Shift and lifestyle

  • Billing roles that call US insurers often work US hours, which means evening or night shifts in Egypt.
  • Coding is more often flexible or day-based, but productivity targets (charts per hour) can be strict.

Long-term growth

  • Billing: AR team lead, denial management, RCM manager, credentialing, contract work for US practices, or running your own billing service.
  • Coding: specialty coder, auditor, coding educator, or clinical documentation improvement (CDI).

My honest advice

  • You like quiet, detailed work and are ready to study hard for the CPC → coding.
  • You like solving problems, talking to insurers and seeing results in money collected → billing. Add the CPB to grow from a local job into dollar-paid contract work.
  • Either way, learn the basics of both. Coders who understand denials and billers who understand codes are the ones who get hired.
