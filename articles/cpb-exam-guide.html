CPB exam · Study guide

CPB exam 2026–2027: what it covers and how to prepare

Format, passing score, the 7 exam domains with question counts, and a simple 8-week study plan.

OptimalPulse Academy · Updated November 2026

Quick answer: The CPB exam has 135 multiple-choice questions, takes 4 hours in one sitting, and you need 70% (95 correct) to pass. You can bring the current-year CPT, ICD-10-CM and HCPCS Level II code books. The biggest parts of the exam are case analysis and types of insurance.

What is the CPB?

The Certified Professional Biller (CPB®) is AAPC's credential for people who work on the money side of a medical practice: claims, payments, denials, appeals and patient collections. Coders turn the visit into codes. Billers make sure those codes turn into payment.

If you already work at a front desk, in posting or in A/R, the CPB is a way to prove that you understand the whole billing cycle, not just your part of it.

What is on the exam: the 7 domains

Here is how the 135 questions are spread across the domains, so you know where to spend your study time.

DomainQuestionsShareWhat it tests
Case analysis3425%Real billing scenarios: read the situation and choose the right action, code or claim fix
Types of insurance2921%Medicare, Medicaid, commercial, managed care, workers' comp, TRICARE, coordination of benefits
Reimbursement & collections1914%Fee schedules, EOBs and remittances, adjustments, patient balances, collections
Claims & billing1914%The CMS-1500 claim, timely filing, rejections and denials, corrected claims, appeals
Billing regulations1713%Fraud and abuse laws, the No Surprises Act, ABNs, payer rules
Coding107%Using CPT, ICD-10-CM and HCPCS correctly, modifiers, medical necessity
HIPAA & compliance75%Privacy and security rules, minimum necessary, compliance programs
Why this matters: case analysis and insurance types together are almost half the exam. If your time is short, start there.

An 8-week study plan

About one hour a day is enough for most people with some billing exposure. Complete beginners should add a few weeks.

WeekFocus
1–2Types of insurance. Learn who pays first, how Medicare parts work, and what makes Medicaid, managed care and workers' comp different.
3Claims and billing. Walk through a CMS-1500 box by box. Learn timely filing limits and the difference between a rejection and a denial.
4Reimbursement and collections. Read sample EOBs, separate contractual adjustments from patient responsibility, and practice balance math.
5Billing regulations and HIPAA. Focus on what a biller must and must not do, and why.
6Coding with your books. Practice looking things up fast: index first, then the tabular list or code section.
7Case analysis practice. Do mixed scenario questions and write one line on why each wrong answer is wrong.
8A full 135-question practice exam under timed conditions, then review every miss.

Exam-day tips

  • Budget your time. 4 hours for 135 questions is about 1 minute 45 seconds each. Skip long ones, mark them and come back.
  • Use your books, but not for everything. Looking up every question will run out the clock. Save the books for codes and modifiers.
  • Read the last line first. In case questions, the question is at the end. Read it first, then the scenario.
  • Answer every question. A blank is always wrong.
  • Check the book rules on AAPC's site before exam day, so nothing gets taken away at check-in.

Common mistakes

  • Studying only coding. Coding is just 7% of the CPB.
  • Mixing up who pays first when a patient has two plans.
  • Treating every zero payment as a denial. Many are deductible or a bundled service.
  • Never doing a full timed practice exam before the real one.
