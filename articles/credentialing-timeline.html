How long does provider credentialing take? (2026 timeline)

Typical times for Medicare, Medicaid and commercial payers, a backwards plan from the start date, and the delays you can prevent.

OptimalPulse Academy · Updated November 2026

Quick answer: Plan for 90 to 120 days from a complete application to being able to bill commercial payers. Medicare is often faster (about 30 to 60 days); Medicaid ranges from about 2 weeks to 3 months or more, depending on the state. Start at least 120 days before the provider's first day.

Typical processing times by payer

These are planning figures for a clean, complete application, not guarantees. Every payer and state is different, so track your own real times.

Payer typeTypical time
Medicare (PECOS)About 30 to 60 days; longer if the contractor sends a development request or orders a site visit
Medicaid (state)About 2 weeks to 3 months or more, depending on the state
Commercial and managed careAbout 60 to 120 days, plus time to load the provider into the payer's system
Hospital privilegesAbout 60 to 120 days

What slows credentialing down

  • Incomplete or stale CAQH. CAQH must be re-attested every 120 days. An old attestation stops the payer's review.
  • Mismatched data. The name, address or taxonomy in NPPES doesn't match the application. One wrong digit in an NPI or tax ID can cost weeks.
  • Unexplained work-history gaps. Gaps of six months or more are almost always questioned. Add a short signed explanation up front.
  • Expired or soon-to-expire documents, such as a license, DEA registration or malpractice certificate.
  • Old signatures and verifications. Under current accreditation standards, verifications can't be more than 120 days old when the plan decides. A half-finished file that sits for weeks may have to be redone.
  • Slow answers to payer requests. Reply to any request within two business days.
  • Duplicate Medicare enrollments. If the provider is already enrolled with the same Medicare contractor, update the existing record instead of starting a new one.

A follow-up rhythm that works

  1. Day 7 to 10: confirm the payer received the application and that it is complete. Ask for the current processing time and a reference number.
  2. Every 14 days after that: check the status. Check sooner if a promised date has passed.
  3. Any request from the payer: answer within two business days, and log the date and the person you spoke with.

Can the provider see patients before approval?

It depends on the payer. For Medicare, the effective date for physicians and practitioners is the later of the date the contractor received an application that was later approved and the date the provider started seeing patients at the location. Medicare then allows billing for services up to 30 days before that effective date, if all requirements were met. Most commercial payers do not pay for visits before their effective date, so those patients should usually be held or rescheduled.

Why this matters: visits seen before a commercial effective date are often never paid. Credentialing is revenue work, not paperwork.
