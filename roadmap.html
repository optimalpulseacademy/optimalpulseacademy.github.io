Why it matters on the job: Every job in the revenue cycle starts from one question: who pays for this visit, and under which rules? Get this wrong and nothing after it works.

What employers expect you to know

  • Payer types: Medicare (Parts A, B, C, D), Medicaid, commercial plans, TRICARE, workers' comp and personal injury
  • Plan types: HMO, PPO and EPO, referrals and prior authorization
  • Patient cost-sharing: deductible, copay and coinsurance, and who owes what
  • HIPAA: protecting patient information in every email, call and file

Official sources

  • Medicare.gov: Medicare basics
  • Medicaid.gov
  • HealthCare.gov: insurance glossary
  • HHS.gov: HIPAA

Quick self-check

A plan pays 80% after the deductible and the patient pays the other 20%. What is that 20% called?

Coinsurance is a percentage of the allowed amount. A copay is a fixed dollar amount, and a premium is what the patient pays to keep the plan.

Which US law protects the privacy of patients' health information?

HIPAA's Privacy Rule sets the rules for protecting patient health information. Everyone in the revenue cycle handles it daily.

How we help: From Zero to CPB: chapter 1 (Types of insurance) and chapter 6 (HIPAA and compliance).

Why it matters on the job: Billers don't assign codes, but they read them on every claim, denial and EOB. If you can't read a code line, you can't find what went wrong.

What employers expect you to know

  • Medical terminology basics: prefixes, roots and suffixes
  • ICD-10-CM (why the patient was seen) vs CPT (what the provider did)
  • HCPCS Level II: supplies, drugs and some services
  • Common modifiers (such as 25, 59 and 26) and how they change payment

Official sources

  • MedlinePlus (NIH): understanding medical words, free
  • CMS: ICD-10 codes and official guidelines
  • AMA: about CPT
  • CMS: HCPCS Level II

Quick self-check

A patient is seen for a sprained ankle. Which code set tells the payer WHY the patient was seen?

ICD-10-CM codes are diagnoses: the reason for the visit. CPT describes what was done.

Who maintains HCPCS Level II codes?

CMS maintains HCPCS Level II. The AMA maintains CPT (also called HCPCS Level I).

How we help: From Zero to CPB: chapter 5 (Coding for billers).

Why it matters on the job: Billing is a chain. When you know every link, you can find exactly where a claim broke and fix it at the source.

What employers expect you to know

  • Registration, eligibility and benefits checks before the visit
  • Prior authorization: when it's needed and what happens without it
  • Charge entry, then the claim: CMS-1500 (professional) and UB-04 (facility)
  • Clearinghouse rejections vs payer denials: not the same problem
  • Timely filing limits for each payer

Official sources

  • NUCC: 1500 claim form instructions
  • CMS: Medicare Claims Processing Manual

Quick self-check

A family doctor's office bills an office visit. Which claim form is used?

Professional services go on the CMS-1500 (or its electronic version). UB-04 is for hospitals and facilities; CMS-855I is a Medicare enrollment form.

A claim never reached the payer because the clearinghouse found a missing patient ID. What is this?

A rejection happens before the payer accepts the claim, so you fix it and resubmit. A denial is a decision by the payer after processing.

How we help: From Zero to CPB: chapter 3 (Claims and billing) and chapter 4 (Billing regulations).

Why it matters on the job: Every payment tells a story. Reading it correctly decides what is written off, what goes to the secondary payer and what the patient really owes.

What employers expect you to know

  • Reading EOBs and ERAs line by line
  • Allowed amount, contractual adjustment and patient responsibility
  • Billing the secondary payer with the primary's payment details
  • Patient statements, credit balances and refunds

Official sources

  • X12: claim adjustment and remark code lists
  • HealthCare.gov: insurance glossary

Quick self-check

Allowed amount $100. Copay $25. Deductible already met, no coinsurance. What does the insurance pay?

Allowed $100 minus the $25 copay = $75 from insurance.

Insurance paid the full allowed amount, and the patient had also paid $50 at check-in. What should the office do with the $50 credit?

A patient credit belongs to the patient. Refund it promptly, or apply it only if policy and state rules allow.

What employers expect you to know

  • Corrected claim vs appeal, and their deadlines

Quick self-check

A denial shows group code CO (contractual obligation). Can the practice bill that amount to the patient?

CO means the amount falls on the provider under the contract. PR (patient responsibility) is the group code for amounts the patient owes.

How many levels of appeal does Original Medicare (Parts A and B) have?

Redetermination, reconsideration, the ALJ hearing, the Medicare Appeals Council, then federal court: five levels.

Quick self-check

You called a payer about a denied claim. What must go into the account note?

Good notes let anyone pick up the account. The call reference number is your proof if the payer gives wrong information.

Which tool shows claims grouped by how long they've been unpaid?

The A/R aging report sorts unpaid balances into 0-30, 31-60, 61-90 and 90+ days so you can prioritise.

Quick self-check

A recruiter asks you to explain a CO-45 adjustment. What's the best short answer?

CO-45 means the charge exceeds the fee schedule or contracted amount. The provider writes it off; the patient is not billed for it.

Quick self-check

What does the suffix '-ectomy' mean?

-ectomy means surgical removal (appendectomy). -itis is inflammation, -scopy is viewing with a scope.

A 'laparoscopic' procedure is done...

Laparoscopic means using a scope through small incisions in the abdomen. The approach often changes which code you choose.

Why it matters on the job: Diagnosis codes prove medical necessity. Wrong or vague diagnoses are one of the most common reasons claims are denied.

What employers expect you to know

  • The Alphabetic Index first, then always confirm in the Tabular List
  • Conventions: Excludes1 vs Excludes2, 'code first', 'use additional code'
  • Official guidelines: sequencing, signs and symptoms, and coding to the highest specificity
  • Chapter-specific rules (for example diabetes, injuries, pregnancy)

Official sources

  • CMS: ICD-10 codes and official guidelines

Quick self-check

After finding a term in the ICD-10-CM Alphabetic Index, what must you do?

The official guidelines say never code from the Index alone. The Tabular List holds the full code, notes and extra characters.

An 'Excludes1' note means...

Excludes1 means 'not coded here': the two conditions are never reported together. Excludes2 means the condition isn't included here but may be coded too.

Why it matters on the job: Procedure codes decide how much the provider is paid. Choosing the right code and modifier is the core skill of a coder.

What employers expect you to know

  • E/M office visits: choosing the level by medical decision-making or time
  • Surgery by body system, plus radiology, pathology/lab and medicine sections
  • HCPCS Level II for drugs, supplies and equipment
  • Modifiers (such as 25, 59, 26, TC, LT/RT) and when they're allowed

Official sources

Quick self-check

Which modifier shows a significant, separately identifiable E/M service on the same day as a procedure?

Modifier 25 is added to the E/M code. Modifier 59 marks a distinct procedural service; 26 is the professional component.

Since 2021, office visit E/M levels are chosen by...

For office and outpatient visits, the level is based on medical decision-making or the provider's total time that day.

Why it matters on the job: A correct code can still be denied. Compliance rules decide which codes can be billed together, how many units and for which diagnoses.

What employers expect you to know

  • NCCI procedure-to-procedure edits and when a modifier may bypass them
  • Medically Unlikely Edits (MUEs): maximum units per day
  • Medical necessity: Local and National Coverage Determinations (LCDs/NCDs)
  • Documentation rules: code only what the provider documented

Quick self-check

The provider's note doesn't support a higher-paying code, but the doctor says 'just bill it'. What should a coder do?

Coders code from documentation only. A compliant query to the provider is the right step; upcoding is fraud risk.

What does a Medically Unlikely Edit (MUE) limit?

MUEs set the maximum units a provider would normally report for one patient on one date of service.

What employers expect you to know

  • Learn to move fast in your code books: tabs, notes and highlights (the CPC is open-book)
  • Check every answer and write down why you were wrong
  • Aim for 95% accuracy, the level many employers expect

Quick self-check

Which habit improves coding accuracy the most?

Codes change every year; guidelines and reasoning stay. Reviewing why you were wrong builds the skill employers test.

What does the 'A' in CPC-A mean?

CPC-A means you passed the exam but haven't yet shown the experience AAPC requires to remove the apprentice status.

Quick self-check

A payer approved a provider's credentialing, but no contract is signed yet. Can the practice bill as in-network?

Credentialing checks the provider; contracting puts them in network. Billing in-network starts from the effective date.

Why do effective dates matter?

Services before the effective date are usually treated as out-of-network or denied, so start dates must be tracked carefully.

Quick self-check

Where does a provider get an NPI?

NPIs are issued through NPPES, run for CMS. CAQH stores provider data that many commercial payers use.

A group practice (as an organisation) bills under which NPI type?

Type 1 is for individual providers. Type 2 is for organisations such as group practices and clinics.

How often must a provider re-attest their CAQH ProView profile?

Every 120 days. If the attestation lapses, payers can't use the profile and applications stall.

Quick self-check

Through which online system do providers enroll in Medicare?

PECOS is CMS's online Medicare enrollment system. Paper CMS-855 forms are the alternative.

A doctor joins a group and wants the group to bill Medicare for their services. Which form handles that?

The 855R reassigns the individual's Medicare benefits to the group so the group can bill for them.
